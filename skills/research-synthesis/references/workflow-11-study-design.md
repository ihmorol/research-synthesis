# Workflow 11 - Study design

Propose a feasible study, separating essential choices from optional improvements.

## Parameters and delivery

- Required: research question and constraints: duration, budget, participant/data access,
  available skills, and material ethics/governance restrictions. Ask missing essentials
  in one batch; do not assume unlimited resources.
- A supplied question is enough; prior gap/question workflows are optional.
- Follow the main delivery mode and existing project convention; save only when authorized,
  optionally to `research/<corpus-key>/<question-key>/study-design.md`.
  No manifest or full pipeline is required.

## Procedure

1. Use stable IDs and verified methods extracts where available. Targeted rereading is
   allowed to check adaptation details, measures, source versions, and methods locators.
   If only references/notes are available, label the evidence limitation and proposal;
   do not claim to have verified a published method.
2. Define the intended inference and outcome/estimand or qualitative analytic focus.
   Choose a design that fits the question and constraints; state why, alternatives,
   and the assumptions needed. Temporal ordering alone does not identify cohort causality;
   experiments/quasi-experiments need valid implementation/identification, not just labels.
3. Cite methods passages for adaptations and state exactly what is borrowed or changed:
   sampling logic, recruitment/data selection, instrument/coding, procedure, or analysis.
   Label new choices as proposed, not source findings; do not fabricate methodological citations.
4. Specify recruitment/eligibility or data provenance/access; comparator where relevant;
   measures/coding and timing; procedure; analysis; missingness/attrition handling;
   relevant confounding or sensitivity checks; ethics, consent/privacy, and governance.
   Note when these elements are not applicable rather than imposing one design template.
5. Explain measurement validity for the intended population and transfer limitations.
   A prior instrument's use is not proof of validation. State appropriate pilot/validation
   objectives without inventing a pilot N or claiming a pilot establishes full validity.
6. For qualitative work, justify sampling and analytic adequacy using the declared approach;
   do not impose a universal saturation threshold or a numerical recruitment minimum.
7. Address sample-size/precision/feasibility planning without invented N. Name the required
   calculation and inputs: estimand/effect or precision target, variance/event rate,
   error/power criterion when relevant, clustering/design effect, attrition, and access bounds.
   A published N is context, not a required new-study N; attainable N is a constraint,
   not a substitute for a justified target. Use defensible sensitivity scenarios if requested.
8. Any requested calculation must show sourced inputs, explicit assumptions, and method;
   label its result calculated, not reported. Otherwise leave unresolved inputs/calculations
   as to-dos. No unsupported recruitment target, pilot number, or promised power.
9. Mark essential choices and optional improvements. Give realistic sequence/milestones,
   likely failure points, mitigations, stop/contingency decisions, and residual limitations.
   Do not cut choices essential to valid inference merely to fit an optimistic timeline.

## Output structure

- Question, inference goal, constraints, proposed design, rationale, and alternatives.
- Adaptations with actual methods locators; source access and validation limits.
- Recruitment/data, measures/coding, comparator/timing/procedure, analysis, and ethics plan.
- Essential versus optional choices and a constraint-aware sequence.
- Sample-size/adequacy/feasibility planning with inputs, assumptions, and unresolved to-dos.
- Failure points, mitigations, contingencies, and what the study cannot establish.

## Completion checks

- Choices fit stated constraints and distinguish borrowed methods from proposals.
- No invented study or pilot N; calculations and source statistics are clearly distinct.
- Causal, measurement, ethical, and feasibility limitations are explicit.
- Essential/optional choices and honest contingencies are present.
