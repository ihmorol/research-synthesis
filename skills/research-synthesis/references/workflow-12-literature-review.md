# Workflow 12 - Literature review

Write a thematic synthesis bounded by the supplied corpus and its evidence limitations.

## Parameters and delivery

- Required: question/purpose, discipline/audience, and target length.
- Themes/debates: use the user's choices or propose a corpus-based structure. Ask only
  when a material choice is unresolved; do not require approval of obvious defaults.
- Clarify whether this is a study-motivating review or another synthesis purpose.
- Follow the main delivery mode and existing project convention; save only when authorized,
  optionally to `research/<corpus-key>/<question-key>/literature-review.md`.
  No briefs, manifest, or full pipeline are required.

## Procedure

1. Use stable IDs and verified extracts when relevant; briefs/tables are optional aids.
   Targeted rereading is allowed for cross-cutting themes, evidence/methods context,
   numerical or locator verification, source versions, and unsupported claims.
   Record abstract-only/notes-only/citation-only limits; references alone are not findings.
2. Describe the corpus scope, selection/access limits, and synthesis purpose. Do not call
   this workflow's output `systematic`, comprehensive, exhaustive, or field-wide by default.
   A supplied corpus and themed narrative do not establish a systematic review; that label
   requires a separately documented, justified systematic search/selection/appraisal process.
3. Organize around themes/debates that advance an argument, not a sequence of paper summaries.
   Integrate multiple sources where appropriate; a single-source paragraph is allowed for
   a distinct claim, pivotal study, or sparse evidence if its limited basis is explicit.
4. Per theme, address the claim, evidence, disagreement, methodological qualification,
   and unresolved issue where relevant. Do not force disagreement or a gap where none is
   supported. Preserve construct, population, comparator, timepoint, and estimand differences.
5. Distinguish original results, pooled review estimates, secondary study reports, source
   theory, author interpretation, and proposed synthesis. Reviews may contribute direct
   synthesis evidence; do not present their cited individual-study results as verified originals.
6. Cite substantive source claims with IDs and precise locators, including author conclusions.
   Retain reported statistics accurately with uncertainty and context; identify any requested
   calculated quantity separately. Do not imply independence for overlapping evidence.
7. Match causal wording to identification. Nonsignificant findings do not establish no
   effect, failure to replicate, or a subgroup difference without appropriate comparative
   evidence. Represent unresolved differences and explanations with calibrated language.
8. State unsupported claims and missing evidence rather than padding or inventing coverage.
   Bound gaps to this corpus; field-wide novelty needs separate verification.
9. Close according to purpose: a justified study-motivating gap if applicable, otherwise
   the synthesis conclusion, uncertainties, and implications. Do not invent a user study.
10. Honor target length within the requested tolerance; state how headings/citations are
    counted. If evidence cannot sustain the requested length, explain rather than pad.
    Include an ID-to-full-citation source list when needed for standalone use.

## Output structure

- Scope/purpose and corpus/access limitations.
- Thematic synthesis with source locators, evidence distinctions, and qualifications.
- Purpose-appropriate conclusion, unresolved questions, and bounded implications/gap.
- Source list; explain whether it is outside the word-count target.

## Completion checks

- Synthesis is argumentative/thematic; single-source passages are allowed when warranted.
- Statistics, source attribution, causal language, and null findings are calibrated.
- No unsupported systematic-review label, field-wide claim, or manufactured motivating gap.
- Target length and source access limitations are addressed without padding.
