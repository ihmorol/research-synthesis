# Workflow 09 - Research gaps

Identify only gaps defensible within the supplied corpus and show how to verify them.

## Parameters and delivery

- Required: topic and source scope.
- Default: up to 5 gaps, not a quota; honor a smaller requested maximum. Return fewer
  or none when support is insufficient, explaining the corpus/access limits.
- For feasibility ranking: time, access, skills, and other material constraints.
  Ask only for decision-critical missing constraints; otherwise omit or qualify feasibility ranks.
- Follow the main delivery mode and existing project convention; save only when authorized,
  optionally to `research/<corpus-key>/<question-key>/gaps.md`.
- No conceptual framework, manifest, or previous workflow is required for a standalone request.

## Procedure

1. Use stable IDs and verified extracts; targeted rereading is allowed to confirm methods,
   limitations, findings, source versions, and locators. Separate corpus coverage from
   unread/unavailable material; references alone cannot establish what studies examined.
2. Establish what the corpus supports and what remains unresolved. Avoid treating an
   author's future-work suggestion or an isolated missing topic as a demonstrated field gap.
3. Classify each supported gap, allowing overlapping types:
   - unanswered question within the inspected corpus;
   - inconsistent evidence after accounting for comparability and uncertainty;
   - measurement/validity limitation;
   - untested extension across populations, settings, mechanisms, or conditions;
   - design/identification limitation preventing the needed inference.
4. Cite the studies/passages demonstrating the limitation and their scope. For a corpus
   absence, state the inspected scope and access limits rather than fabricating a passage
   proving that absence. Distinguish established limitations from proposed opportunities.
5. Explain theoretical/practical importance and what resolving the gap would add.
   Self-report alone is not proof of invalidity; nonsignificant results alone are not
   inconsistent evidence or proof of no effect.
6. Use `not addressed in this corpus` or a similarly bounded claim, never `no research
   exists` or `never studied`. Give verification search terms and a plan for checking
   current reviews/original studies; label field-wide novelty `unverified` until checked.
   Do not browse or invoke a search handoff without the user's authorization.
7. Rank by stated importance and feasibility criteria, explaining trade-offs and uncertainty
   without fabricated scores or access assumptions. Distinguish a practical recommendation
   from a claim that a gap has already been established across the field.

## Output fields per gap

- Gap label and type(s).
- Established evidence, unresolved issue, and demonstrating paper IDs/locators.
- Inspected corpus scope and relevant access limitations.
- Why resolution matters and the evidence/study needed.
- Verification search terms, verification status, and optional authorized handoff.
- Rank/rationale under stated constraints, or an explicit limitation on feasibility ranking.

## Completion checks

- At most 5 gaps by default; unsupported slots are not filled.
- Gap scope, sources, importance, verification path, and uncertainty are explicit.
- Absence, disagreement, and measurement weakness are not overclaimed.
- Feasibility recommendations use actual constraints; field-wide novelty remains unverified.
