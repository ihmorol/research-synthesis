# Workflow 10 - Research questions

Turn a chosen gap into answerable questions and clearly labelled testable expectations.

## Parameters and delivery

- Required: gap/problem and target context. A user-supplied gap is sufficient; workflow 09
  is optional. State when its evidence basis or field-wide novelty remains unverified.
- Default: up to 4 questions, not a quota; honor a smaller requested maximum.
- For a final recommendation: degree/project type, duration, and material access/skill constraints.
- Follow the main delivery mode and existing project convention; save only when authorized,
  optionally to `research/<corpus-key>/<question-key>/research-questions.md`.
  No manifest or full pipeline is required.

## Procedure

1. Use stable IDs and verified extracts for source-based rationales. Targeted rereading
   is allowed for theory, findings, methods, source versions, and locator checks.
2. For each distinct question, specify population/unit of analysis, constructs, context,
   intended contribution, and what observations would answer it. Return fewer questions
   if more would be redundant, unsupported, or infeasible.
3. Distinguish exploratory questions from directional hypotheses. A hypothesis may be
   grounded in empirical findings, theory only, or an explicitly proposed synthesis.
   Cite available premises; label theory-only predictions `untested hypothesis` and
   proposed expectations as such. Do not invent an empirical basis or direction.
4. Explain assumptions and scope when extending findings to a new context. Exploratory
   questions need no fabricated directional hypothesis; state what could challenge the
   proposed interpretation or framing instead of demanding a statistical falsifier.
5. Propose a suitable design and a concrete testable challenge to each expected explanation.
   For a quantitative prediction, identify the contrast/estimand and prespecified result
   pattern that would count against it, considering uncertainty and meaningful-effect bounds.
   Nonsignificance, a post-hoc power figure, or preregistration of a null is not proof of no effect.
   Consider equivalence testing only when justified bounds and design support it.
6. Match causal language to identification, not design names. Observational timing alone
   is insufficient; reword unsupported causal questions associationally or state the
   design/assumption upgrade needed. Label proposed mechanisms as untested.
7. If recommendation constraints are available, recommend the strongest feasible option
   with reasons about contribution, access, analysis load, timeline, and risks. If they
   are missing, qualify the recommendation or ask; do not invent access or required Ns.

## Output fields per question

- RQ and exploratory/hypothesis status.
- Population/unit, constructs, context, and intended contribution.
- Hypothesis if appropriate; empirical/theoretical/proposed basis with source locators.
- Suggested design, estimand or analytic focus, and identification limitations.
- Testable challenge/disconfirming observations and uncertainty-aware interpretation.
- Feasibility risks and causal-wording flags.

End with a justified recommendation when requested and supported by stated constraints.

## Completion checks

- At most 4 RQs by default; theory-only hypotheses are allowed but labelled untested.
- Every question is answerable, distinct, and has a suitable challenge to its explanation/framing.
- Causal wording, uncertainty, transfer assumptions, and constraint fit are explicit.
